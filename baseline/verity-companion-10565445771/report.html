The per-slot deficit deepens as the day runs: −24% at 00:00, −31% at 12:00, −38% at 14:30. A defect costs the same share of players in every slot. A deficit that widens through the day is a decline still in progress.

The whole life

The mechanism

Five things happened in order, and the order is the diagnosis.

Roblox scaled it, hard. HomeRecommendation impressions went 266,428 on 13 Aug to 12,522,663 on the 23rd — 47× in ten days. Home is 73% of this game's DAU; its distribution is rented, not owned.

Retention diluted as the marginal viewer got less interested. Blended D1 fell from 24.8% to 12.2%. But it did not fall everywhere.

The ranking gate then collapsed. RFYQualifiedPTR fell 0.0399 to 0.0092, −77%. Impression-to-play conversion went 14.37% to 6.67% over the same stretch.

Roblox withdrew reach on 23 August — impressions −36.9% in two days — and DAU followed within a day.

Impressions and conversion are now falling together, which per Indigo's own playbook means a demotion following an appeal drop: fix appeal first, then re-qualify. Reach work alone will not hold.

The constraint

The fall is the news. This is the story.

Verity converts an unusually good first session into almost no second one. Only 35% of day-1 returners come back on day 2 — against 43–49% on the four games Indigo already runs, which was already the studio's weak spot.

Past day 2 the curve is fine — 66%, 101%, 95%. Whoever survives the second day is genuinely stuck to it. The entire loss is one cliff, one day wide.

Which is exactly what the genre predicts:

  • Session length 8.17 min and falling from a 10.20 peak, and the session-length distribution truncates at 18 minutes. There is no long tail.
  • Public badge data: the welcome badge is awarded to 100% of players; the two ending badges sit at 9.1% and 9.2%. About one player in eleven reaches an ending, and that ratio is stable day over day — a design level, not a regression.
  • RFYL7PlayDays 1.38 against the ~2.0 a promoted game needs. The first six days sum to roughly 25 percentage points; the bar is about 100.
  • The store description states a 40-message cap — a hard per-session ceiling, consistent with both the 18-minute truncation and the day-2 cliff.

There is no offline-earnings, daily-reward or streak mechanic — precisely the class that holds day 1 to day 2, and precisely the day Verity loses.

What the errors say

dashboard export
The core loop, failing half a million times

Generated dialogue was rejected by filtering for <Player> — 512,734 events, server-side, first seen v446.

The AI composed a reply and Roblox's text filter blocked it, so the player asked Verity something and got nothing back. It is by far the largest non-cosmetic signature in the game, and it is the most plausible mechanism behind the 73.85% churn at First Reply Received. A player whose first message returns silence has been shown the product failing.

Two more that are material:

  • Monetize:PromptProductPurchase() called with prompt in progress, aborting — 13,912. A purchase prompt that never appears is revenue lost outright — the same defect class that cost 1,844 prompts on Become a Brainrot.
  • cannot resume non-suspended coroutine at Packages.Packet:316 — 12,570, first seen v609. A networking-library bug a specific deploy introduced, in the transport every feature rides on.

Not material, named so nobody re-reports them: the 64-track animation limit (332,195), TopbarPlus version notices, [DayMusicClient] soundtrack warnings, and the mesh/sound/PBR load failures — cosmetic or self-healing.

Open question — unique to this game

This is the only game in the portfolio with a variable cost per player. The description says the AI is paid for per use: “AI costs money to run, so unlimited free messages may not always be possible.”

Revenue per DAU is R$0.39, about $0.00136 per player per day. If inference costs more than that per player, the game loses money as it grows — and every DAU number on this page reads the opposite way round.

Nobody has told us the unit cost. Ask before recommending any growth spend. I have deliberately not modelled it rather than guess at it.

Run-rate gap from the 21 Aug peak is roughly $36,150/month, about $217k capitalised at the 6× owner-ask multiple. Two caveats, both material: ×30 on single days is a simplification, and part of this fall is the unwind of a spike that was never a sustainable level.

What to do

There is no defect to fix, so every lever here is a design one. Ranked by expected size.

  1. Build a reason to open it tomorrow. Target cohort day 2, currently 4.91% at a 35% hold. The lever class that holds this exact day is a daily reset — a fresh message allowance, a daily scenario, a streak. Getting the hold from 35% to the 48% the rest of the portfolio manages is a 20–50% move on one step, inside the believable band. Read it two days after ship, mix-controlled.
  2. Fix the filtered-dialogue path. 512,734 blocked replies. Either retry with a re-composed reply, or ship a graceful in-character fallback so the player never meets silence. Measure on step-4 churn, currently 73.85%.
  3. Do not spend on reach yet. Impressions and conversion are falling together; the playbook's own table says fix appeal first. Reach bought at 6.67% conversion into a 35% day-2 hold leaks straight back out.
  4. Get the AI unit cost from the studio, then re-read the money section against it.
  5. Pull the player-comment report — the one export still missing. Comments named the mechanism twice on Become a Brainrot when the metrics could not, and comment text is not retrievable from the API at all.
