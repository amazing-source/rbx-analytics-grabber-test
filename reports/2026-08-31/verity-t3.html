One correction to the day-2 number

Read alone, 5.75% → 4.46% looks like a fall the update caused. It is not. Across seven cohorts DailyCohortRetention CohortDay 2 has sat between 4.46% and 5.76%, and the day-2-over-day-1 ratio between 30% and 42%, with no trend either way. The 28 Aug cohort at 35.1% is mid-range for this game.

The problem is the level, not the movement. Every other game in the portfolio holds 43–49% of its day-1 players into day 2. Verity has never held more than 42%, on any cohort we hold.

What is actually happening

Roblox ranks a game for the Recommended-For-You surface on a set of signals about how people actually play it. We pulled all twenty for the first time on 31 Aug, and they split cleanly at the boundary between the first session and the second.

So the problem is not appeal, and it is not the build. People arrive, enjoy one session, and never come back. There is nothing in the game that asks them to — no daily reward, no offline progress, no reason to open it tomorrow rather than next week.

This matters beyond retention. Roblox states that an impressions decline typically follows a fall in play days and playtime per user. RFYD2To7PlayDays is down 36% and still falling, so we should expect less traffic in the coming weeks unless it turns. The fall starts 27 August, the day before the update — v679 did not cause it and will not fix it.

Stated confound

Play-days-per-user is diluted by new players, and DailyActiveUsers rose 50% across this window. Against that reading: the signal held 0.38–0.40 on 24–26 Aug at higher DAU — 845K on 24 Aug — and kept falling on 29 Aug. Six days, one game, no prior baseline: strong enough to plan against, not yet strong enough to call a trend. The next grab confirms it or kills it.

What we want to do about it

Priority 1

Give players a reason to come back tomorrow

daily reward + offline accrual, both paid in messages

Two mechanics, both small: a daily login reward of messages, and offline accrual — roughly one message per 30 minutes away, capped at about 15. The cap is the important part: it fills in about seven hours, so it rewards coming back tomorrow and gives nothing to someone who leaves the game open.

This is aimed squarely at the day-2 gap. Verity is the only game in the portfolio with no daily-reset mechanic at all, and the only one holding under 43% into day 2.

The catch, and it is a real one

Messages are almost the entire business, so giving them away is giving away the product. This is the thing to get right, and it is worth being precise about how much room there is.

How much cannibalisation we can afford

Two facts make the trade better than it first looks. PayingUsersCVR is 0.79%, so 99.2% of players never spend anything — free messages to them cost nothing. And 61% of revenue is GamePass, a permanent unlock a daily grant does not substitute for; only the 39% that is DevProduct message packs is exposed. Sized against 30 Aug: DAU 535,950, revenue R$255,562, of which DevProduct is R$99,505.

The upside is modelled from this game’s own measured cohort curve. 1 + Σ(D1..D6) reproduces RFYL7PlayDays to within 0.7% — predicted 1.251 against a measured 1.243 — so the model is validated on Verity before being used on it.

That upside is real but unsized; do not promise it.

How to build it safely

Ship the grant as a dial, not a number

start at 8 messages · raise toward 15 only while the guardrail holds

Roblox’s Configs API changes values like this without publishing an update — it propagates in about five minutes, keeps a version history, and rolls back instantly. Put the grant size behind it rather than hard-coding it.

Start conservative at 8 messages, hold 48 hours, and raise toward 15 only while the guardrail holds. If it moves the wrong way, it is one config change to undo — not a rollback.
