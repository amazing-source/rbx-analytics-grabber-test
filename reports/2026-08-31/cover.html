The tutorial is losing our best customers

Cash packs beat their target three times over — DailyRevenue +19.3%, PayingUsers +36%. But 39 in 100 new players never reach a driveway, four in ten quit inside three minutes, and net rating per player is down 84%. We now know why, and it is the tutorial.

The two games are failing in opposite places

Roblox ranks a game for the Recommended-For-You surface on a set of signals about how people actually play it. We pulled all twenty for the first time on 31 August, and they say these two games have inverted problems.

Verity’s front door works and its back door is open. Seal’s is the reverse. They need opposite fixes: Verity needs a reason to return tomorrow, Seal needs the first three minutes to land. A single “improve retention” instruction would send both studios at the wrong half of their own problem.

This also tells us what to expect. Roblox reduces a game’s promotion after these signals fall, not before — so on both games the traffic movement is a consequence of the product, and more of it should be expected unless the underlying signal turns.

Seal’s cause is no longer a guess

At the two-day read we said the fix had to wait on player feedback. It does not. The defect report read against v908 traced ten faults to source, and two of them — no desktop performance preset and an unbounded squeegee sweep — land squarely on the first minutes. Two more are known from playing the build and are not in that report: the mobile tutorial UI is too large, and the tutorial takes about three minutes to reach the core verb. That last one and the 39.6% who quit inside three minutes are the same event described from opposite sides.

One thing we had wrong, and it changes urgency

We previously told you that retention work takes about four weeks to show up in Roblox’s promotion, because the signal window is 28 days. That was our inference and Roblox’s own documentation contradicts it — the ranking does not wait for a full 28 days, and shorter-term signals count from a player’s first sessions.

Practically: a fix shipped this week starts counting this week. There is no reason to pace these against a late-September horizon.
