LiveOps diagnosis/Verity Companion & Seal A Driveway/30 days to 2026-08-30

Eight Days Behind

Verity's revenue is down 53% from its peak, and the cause is not the update — it is a retention decline that Roblox's recommender responded to by withdrawing 61% of the game's impressions. The same thing happened to Monkey and Waterslide. Seal is showing the same signature about eight days later, while posting its best revenue day ever.

Sections 04–07 cover why this keeps recurring and what actually moves it, including the two ranking signals that changed on 15 June and the one number capping both games.

Roblox's algorithm did not penalise the game. It read the engagement signals and reallocated impressions — which is what it is built to do. D7 retention sits at 1.1–1.9%: after a week, essentially nobody is left. No amount of acquisition survives that, and Verity was running at 60–86% new users every single day, so the moment the tap closed the game had almost no base to fall back on.

One thing this chain is not

An earlier draft of this document framed it as a same-week trigger: D1 drops below ~12.5%, traffic gets cut the next day. Across all five games that association is real — median next-day home-recommendation change is −19.7% after a sub-12.5% day versus +3.3% otherwise — but it does not survive scrutiny as a mechanism, for two reasons.

First, D1 for a given day cannot settle until two days later, so nothing can act on it overnight. Second, on Verity and Seal the low-D1 days were their single largest traffic days (same-day correlation between home-recommendation volume and D1 is −0.44 and −0.69), so a surge depresses D1 and then mean-reverts. Since 2026-06-15 Roblox ranks on a 28-day signal window, which means the withdrawal reflects weeks of accumulated engagement, not one bad day.

Treat sub-12.5% D1 as a marker that the withdrawal is already underway, not as a tripwire you can stay above by watching it daily.

What does survive is stronger, and it is the finding that matters: the retention decline is in the product, not the traffic mix. Home-recommendation share held flat at 70–78% across the whole window while D1 fell on every acquisition source — Home 28.0% → 12.5%, Friends 19.8% → 11.3%, Others 19.2% → 11.3%. Only Search held (19.9% → 17.4%), which is what you would expect: people who searched for the game by name still want it.

That is Roblox's own discrimination test for exactly this question, and it comes back product. The game got worse at holding people; it was not handed worse people.

The unsettling-AI conceit is presumably deliberate. It is the most plausible driver of the retention fall that started the chain in section 01.

The comparison worth taking seriously

Early warning

The same product signature, eight days later Watch

Seal's D1 is falling on every acquisition source, exactly as Verity's did — Home 16.3% → 11.7%, Friends 11.1% → 7.9%, Others 13.0% → 10.3%, Search 24.5% → 15.5% — while home-recommendation share held at 76–83%. This is not dilution from the traffic surge. The game is holding fewer of everyone.

Seal reached 11.2% D1 on 08-28, below where Verity was when its impressions were withdrawn, and its home-recommendation DAU fell 29% on 08-29. That single day proves nothing on its own — but the underlying product deterioration is measured, not inferred, and it is the input Roblox ranks on.

The difference in Seal's favour: its reach is still expanding (impressions 279k → 4.9M since 08-14) and its click-to-play conversion is holding near 10%, comfortably inside the strong band. Verity's conversion fell from 14.4% to 5.4% while its impressions collapsed — both halves failing at once. Seal has appeal; what it is losing is the ability to hold the people appeal brings.

What is genuinely different, and good Steady

Seal's returning cohort is compounding hard: 997 daily returners on 08-16, 104,103 on 08-29, and it grew +24% on 08-29 while new users fell 31%. Verity's returning cohort never exceeded 24% of its DAU and fell 53% during the cut.

Seal's D7 is 2.1–2.3% against Verity's 1.1–1.9%. Both are low, but Seal's return loop is working in a way Verity's never did. That is the asset to protect.

Onboarding is not the problem Steady

Started 100% → ReachedDriveway 97.4% → PouredOut 95.6% → FirstPass 94.0% → DrivewaySealed 94.0% → Complete 82.9%, and stable across every weekly window since 08-14. Whatever is pulling D1 down, players are not failing to get into the game.

Player-reported defects that cost sessions Act

Flagged only because these are player-loss mechanisms rather than polish — you are already inventorying the rest:

  • Progress loss — "doesn't save progress at all"; one player reports reaching 91% of the first map, disconnecting, and losing everything.
  • Equipment lock — equipping the bucket prevents switching to the squeegee and back; resetting the character does not clear it. A dead progression sink.
  • Early session kicks — "I keep getting kicked out of the game at least a minute after I join."

Any of the three would depress D1 directly, which is the metric that matters most right now.

04Why the algorithm keeps doing this

It has happened to Monkey and Waterslide as well. The reason is the same each time, and it is not arbitrary.

On 15 June 2026 Roblox re-weighted Recommended-For-You to value long-term retention. Two changes matter here:

  • The signal window went from 7 days to 28 days, split across D1, D2–7 and D8–28.
  • QPTR was removed from ranking entirely, replaced by play-through rate and first-play bounce rate (under 60s, and 61–180s).

So the thing being ranked is now, in Roblox's own terms: play days per user, playtime per user (capped at 60 min/day), play-through rate and bounce rate — all as per-user averages, and counting only organically-discovered users. Paid, social and search-acquired players are excluded from the ranking stage entirely.

The ceiling nobody is looking at

RFYL7PlayDays — play days per user over seven, which since June is the ranking input rather than a proxy for it — reads 1.28 on Verity and 1.32 on Seal. Both are drifting down.

That means the average player opens the game roughly once or twice a week and never more. Every reach improvement is multiplied by that number, so it is the hard ceiling on distribution for both titles. A game that cannot move it will be cut again after the next surge, whatever else is fixed.

Two consequences to plan around. Retention work reaches full ranking effect about four weeks after it ships, because the window is 28 days — so a fix landing this week is a late-September distribution story, not a next-week one. And QPTR work is no longer distribution work. Both games already convert well above target (Seal ~10%, Verity 5.4–7.0% against a 3% bar), so thumbnail and icon iteration is not the lever here; it would not have prevented any of these cuts.

Verity — the return loop was never built

Structural

DauMauStickiness fell from 0.240 to 0.055 between 08-21 and 08-28 — the average monthly player now opens the game about 1.7 days a month. A companion game has a natural ending: the Ending funnel exists, 44.8% of onboarded players complete it, and after that there is no second goal. That is why D7 is 1.1% and why the 28-day signal is weak no matter how good the first session is.

The fix is a reason to return tomorrow, not a better first session. In order of expected effect:

  • A daily beat the companion owns — something Verity remembers and continues, so the return is narrative rather than a login bonus. This directly targets play-days-per-user, which is the ranking input.
  • Fix the 10→20 minute session cliff. Retention 59.6% at 10 minutes, 26.9% at 20 — completely flat before it, then half gone. Whatever ends sessions there also caps playtime-per-user, the other ranking signal.
  • Soften the account-metadata callouts. A third of downvotes are fear, and "I am never playing again" is a D1 mechanism, not a sentiment problem.
  • Restore the MessagePack prompt (broken 08-07) and the Notebook decay. Revenue, not retention — but it is three weeks of paywall traffic reaching nothing.

Seal — the loop exists and is being rejected

Tuning + defects

Seal has the structure Verity lacks — a rebirth ladder — and its returning cohort is compounding (997 → 104,103 daily returners). It also has three defects that destroy first sessions outright, and those come first because they are the cheapest points of D1 available:

  • Progress loss on disconnect — a player reports reaching 91% of the first map and losing everything. A player who loses a session's work does not return on day 2.
  • Equipment lock — bucket and squeegee cannot be swapped, and resetting does not clear it. This is a dead progression sink: the player cannot continue at all.
  • Kicks within a minute of joining — this lands directly on first-play bounce rate, which since June is an explicit ranking signal.

Then the tuning question. Feedback says "Rebirth slop — just let me play my game", and session length has fallen 21.6 → 15.0 min while ARPPU fell 17%. The ladder is doing its job on the people who clear it; the complaint is about the distance to the first rung. That is a balance number, not a build — see the lever below.

What is realistic

A 20–50% improvement in a retention or conversion metric is believable with a named mechanism behind it. Anything past 2× that needs a specific reason, and none of the fixes here justifies one.

Concretely: Seal's three defects are worth a D1 point or two if they affect the share of sessions the feedback volume implies — that is a real number, and enough to matter at the ranking margin. Verity's daily-return beat is the only item on this page that could plausibly move RFYL7PlayDays off 1.28, and it is a build, not a config. Nothing here restores 1.28M DAU; the honest ceiling is holding what the next surge delivers instead of shedding it in four days.

Both need someone who plays the game; per house rule, no level judgement here has survived that test and none should be shipped as if it had.

What would overturn this. If Verity's D1 recovers to 18%+ without any product change, the retention-drives-distribution reading is wrong and the cause is external. If Seal's impressions keep climbing through next week while its D1 stays near 11%, the eight-days-behind comparison fails and should be dropped rather than defended.
