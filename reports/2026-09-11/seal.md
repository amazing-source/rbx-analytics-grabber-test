## Where this sits on the lifecycle

Seal peaked **2026-08-28 at 533,776 DAU** — six days before the update — and is
now at **11% of peak**. It is the freshest peak in the pack and the steepest
descent from one.

**A caveat that limits this document, stated plainly.** The trend-residual
instrument used for the other games needs a post-peak run-up to fit against.
Seal's peak is 08-28 and the publish is 09-04, leaving **four non-Saturday days**.
A four-point fit has almost no residual spread, so the z-scores it produced were
absurd (127, 38, 26) and are **not reported**. Everything above is the fit-free
matched-weekday comparison. Any claim here that Seal departed from "its own
trend" would be an artifact, so no such claim is made.

## Order of movement

From the raw daily series rather than a fit:

- Volumes fell continuously from the 08-28 peak, through the update, without an
  inflection at the publish: DAU 533,776 (08-28) → 111,643 (09-04) → 57,930 (09-08).
- The update produced a small, brief lift in concurrents (09-03 2,274 → 09-04
  2,554 → 09-05 2,575) against a falling DAU.
- **Engagement rates moved up across the window**, not down.
- **`RFYD2To7PlayDays` −19.0% and D2–7 qualified sessions −22.7%** are the two
  worst rate moves on the game.

## Interpretation

*Separated deliberately. The sections above are measurements; this one is not.*

**Confirmed — the first session improved, on several independent instruments.**
Bounce <60s **−12.1%**, RFY D1 playtime **+9.3%**, D1 qualified sessions +5.9%,
session length +0.7%, and the early funnel moved substantially:

| Step | Before | After | Δpp |
|---|---|---|---|
| `Complete` | 67.9% | 73.9% | **+6.0** |
| `FirstUpgrade` | 83.2% | 87.8% | **+4.6** |
| `FirstPass` | 87.4% | 90.8% | **+3.4** |
| `DrivewaySealed` | 82.3% | 84.6% | +2.3 |
| `FirstTruck` | 7.8% | 9.7% | +1.9 |
| `PouredOut` | 93.0% | 94.4% | +1.5 |

Six steps improving together, with onboarding already at 100%, is a real
first-session gain rather than noise.

**Confirmed — D1 held on every acquisition source.** Friends −1.3%,
HomeRecommendation **+3.2%**, Search −2.7%. Retention flat-to-up while reach fell
−51% is the documented signature of a **distribution change, not a product
regression** — and unlike Monkey, `Friends` did not fall, so the game did not get
worse for people arriving by invite.

**Likely — but the reach cut is not necessarily external.** The method requires
finishing this test rather than stopping at "retention is flat". D1 is one of
eight ranking signals, and two of the others fell hard: **D2–7 play days −19.0%**
and **D2–7 qualified sessions −22.7%**. Roblox states an impression decline
typically follows a fall in play days per user. So the reading is: the game got
better at the *first* session and worse at getting people *back*, and the
long-horizon signal is the one distribution ranks on.

**Likely — the deep progression is still dead, and that is where the return loop
goes.** `Airport` sits at **0.2%** completion and `ParkingGarage` at **0.0%**,
against `Complete` at 73.9%. That is consistent with the coverage blocker already
established on this game (the server discards finished roads below 0.93 coverage,
which blocks the airport and therefore rebirth) — a bug you named previously, so
I am referencing it rather than introducing it.

**Confirmed — monetization improved.** ARPU +14.5%, paying CVR +26.2%. But note
the survivorship caution: on a game losing 69% of DAU, per-player averages rise
partly because the marginal player leaves first.

**Ruled out:** an in-game first-session regression (six funnel steps up, bounce
down); creative (Friends flat, Home D1 up); weekday shape (matched windows).

## What this does not establish

- **No trend-relative claim** — the fit is unusable here; see the caveat above.
- **The size of the return-loop cost is not measurable from aggregates**, because
  a player blocked at the airport leaves the denominator of every per-player
  average. Breadth is established; depth is not.
- **v915 is unread.** Anything from yesterday or today is out of scope.
