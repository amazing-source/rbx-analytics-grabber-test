The CCU is real, and it is not Seal's fault

Concurrents are down 53% on matched time-of-day slots. Four of five portfolio games hit their lowest reading on record today, so the fall is not this game breaking. What is Seal's own is the first session — and the mobile tutorial sits on top of the pour button.

The short version

CCU is genuinely low, and it is not normal — but it is not a Seal event. Today's mean over the 29 elapsed half-hour slots is 2,111 against a 7-day baseline of 4,539: −53.5%. On the same window Brainrot is −45.2%, Banana Monkey −36.9% and Waterslide −28.2% — and all three are at rank 27 of 27, their lowest reading in the whole series. Verity alone holds at −7.6%. Five games do not break the same morning.

The public board did not move nearly enough to explain it. Combined CCU across the discovery sorts is 7,739,431 today against 7,537,949 on 08-25 at a comparable hour — up 2.7%. Excluding Steal An Egg, which now holds 21.6% of the entire board (12.2% on 08-25), the rest of the board is down about 10% since 08-28. Our four are down 29–54%. The board explains a slice, not the fall.

Nothing in the v908 build is breaking the game. ClientCrashRate15m has fallen every day (0.53% → 0.42%), OomUnexpectedExits 135 → 80, ClientFpsP10 45.3 → 48.3. The errors-per-CCU spike of 08-30 cleared on its own. Revenue rose every day through 08-30 and PayingUsers is up 36% since the publish.

Seal's own problem is the first session, it is getting worse daily, and the mechanism is now named in source. 41.6% of Recommended-For-You first plays end inside three minutes, up from 34.5% a week ago. The tutorial card is the only major UI in the game that does not shrink with the viewport, and on a phone it is drawn on top of the pour button.

The per-slot deficit is a bowl, not a step

Against the median of the last 14 days, the deficit runs −22% at 00:00, deepens to −52% at 06:30, then lifts back to −30% to −35% across the whole afternoon — the last four slots read −30%, −32%, −33%, −35%. A break inside the game costs the same share of players in every slot. A deficit that deepens through the UTC small hours and recovers as the main play hours arrive is a discovery-and-audience shape, and it appeared on several unrelated games at once.

The headline −53.5% is therefore harder on the game than the live picture is. It averages a very deep overnight deficit into a much shallower afternoon one. Judged only on the hours that carry the audience, Seal is running about a third below its recent normal, not a half.

One honest limit on the board comparison. The platform snapshots were taken at different times of day — 08-25 at 14:33Z, 08-28 at 15:31Z, today at 13:35Z. The 08-29 snapshot at 11:17Z is not comparable and is excluded. Board CCU moves by hour, so treat the 2.7% and −4.5% as approximate. They do not need to be precise to carry the point: nothing on the board moved 50%.

You asked whether the metrics really say players leave after about three minutes. They do, and it is worse than when the defect report said it. That is a ranking spiral, not a coincidence. 4 · The eleventh fault not in the defect report

Critical New finding UIKit:94–95 · TutorialClient:930–943 · DrivewayClient:1416–1426

The tutorial card does not scale to the screen, and on a phone it covers the pour button

Reports, this week alone: “The tutorial blocks the entire bottom half of the screen”; “Why is the tut all in my face cant see or click on what im supposed to”; “The tutoria in front of the inventory box makes it difficult to start play and confusing”; “It told me to press pour and pressed pour about 50 times and it still wouldn't work”; “couldnt even pour out the sealer”; “I couldn't see what I was doing when doing the tutorial because it's so big and in my view of what I'm doing; maybe make it smaller?”

The HUD has a viewport fit scale. The tutorial is not on it. UIKit and Shell both compute a shared scale that shrinks every block down to 0.45× on a small screen. TutorialClient requires UIKit for styling helpers but never subscribes to the fit hook — there is no onFit and no fitNow anywhere in the file. Its two UIScale objects are fixed constants: cardScale is an animation pop at 0.94, bannerScale is a flat 0.6.

+ The shape of the fix

-- 1. Put the tutorial on the same scale as everything else.
UIKit.onFit(function(fit, vp)
  cardScale.Scale = fit          -- not a fixed 0.94
  bannerScale.Scale = 0.6 * fit  -- not a fixed 0.6
end)

-- 2. Never let the card enter the touch-control corner. The controls own
--    a fixed 240 x 146 px box at the bottom right; reserve it.
local CTRL_BOX = Vector2.new(RAIL_CLEAR + POUR_PX, POUR_PX + CTRL_BOTTOM)
-- clamp the card's bottom edge above vp.Y - CTRL_BOX.Y whenever
-- UserInputService.TouchEnabled, or dock it to the top on touch.

-- 3. On the narrow branch, dock rather than centre: the card belongs
--    at the top of a phone screen, not across the middle of it.
Why this is worth more than its severity chip suggests. It sits on the majority platform, at the exact step the funnel loses most of its people (Started → ReachedDriveway, 38% lost), and it converts a tutorial into a wall. It is also the cheapest thing on this page to fix — three small changes in one file, no data migration, no balance implications.

One thing this read could not settle

The largest game-owned error introduced by v908 is DrivewayClient:2924: invalid argument #3 to 'clamp', 3,933 events, first seen at V908. It cannot be diagnosed from the dev place. Line 2924 there is math.clamp(rigState.tank / (rigState.tankMax * 0.12), 0.45, 1) — literal bounds, which can never throw that error. Every other clamp in the file was checked and every bound is a safe constant: SEALER_MAX_SKIP 3, SEALER_FRAME_MAX 3, ENDLESS_MAX_PTS 16, Config.PourTiltYawMax 65.

So the live v908 build and this dev place differ in DrivewayClient, even though they agree at lines 179, 906, 1596 and 2908. Settling it needs the live place opened, or the v908 source. Three CoreGui render-step signatures at V901 account for 47,163 events — 74% of the v890–v908 export — and are Roblox's own code, though something in ours is likely provoking them.

6 · What to do ranked by mechanism, not by event count

  1. Put the tutorial on the shared fit scale and keep it out of the touch-control corner. One file, three changes. It sits on the majority platform at the step that loses 38% of starts, and it is the only finding here that plausibly moves the play-through rate this week.
  2. Turn on Mesh & Image APIs. Game Settings → Security. One checkbox, needs a republish, still producing 1,305 events across three signatures at v890–v908. Free.
  3. Fix the tank capacity recompute (fault 1). Still the only Critical on the board, still unfixed, and it is a paid-for upgrade that visibly does nothing until the player re-summons.
  4. Investigate the DataStore write queue. 238,793 dropped-request warnings and five independent player reports of lost progress. This is a save-loss mechanism and it is not in the defect report.
  5. Get the live v908 source for DrivewayClient, or open the live place, so the 3,933-event clamp error introduced by v908 can be located. The dev place cannot answer it.
  6. Add the desktop quality preset (fault 3) and the blade stride (fault 4). Both High, both confirmed, both still costing a good PC its frame rate. Lower priority than the tutorial only because they cost more to do and affect fewer first sessions.
The publish hold, and the tension in it. The standing decision was to ship nothing before 2026-09-05, because ForwardD7Retention for the D3/D7 rework can only be read that day and a new PlaceVersion would sit across it. That hold was set when CCU was 5,000 and rising. It is now 2,111 and falling for the fifth day. Shipping the tutorial fix costs one confounded retention read; not shipping it costs four more days at 41.6% three-minute bounce. The recommendation is to ship it and accept the confound — but that is a call for the producer, not for this report, and it is the only place here where the two standing rules genuinely conflict.
