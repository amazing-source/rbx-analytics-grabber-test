02

What actually survived: quality holds under expansion

The one clean, repeated result across the whole study. When Roblox scales an experience, the marginal player does not behave worse on eight of eleven games. Bounce falls, playtime rises, play-days rise. Only PTR dilutes — which Roblox documents as normal during expansion.

Waterslide is the sharpest case: it lost 79.8% of its Home reach while D8–28 play days moved −2.8%, PTR rose 3.4% and first-play bounce improved 9.6%. Everything Roblox says it ranks on was flat or better through an 80% withdrawal.

Three games read as "fails at scale" and I do not believe the label. Poison Treats, Plane Training and Don't Wake Police are the three oldest games, whose RFY series exist only during their long decline tail. In a shrinking game the survivors are more committed, so engagement per player rises as reach falls — producing exactly the negative correlation the test reads as failure. That is core-hardening, the mirror image of dilution, and this test cannot separate them. unresolved

04

Where the money is: acquisition

Home recommendation is 59–83% of DAU on every one of the twelve games. That dependency is the portfolio's defining structural fact, and it is not a lever anyone controls.

Search is the highest-quality channel on almost every game — best D7, best 7-day playtime, best 30-day revenue per user — and it is 0.1–6% of new users. On Knife Farm, Search delivers D7 of 4.35% against Home's 3.64% and R$6.41 per user against R$3.71. confirmed

A discrepancy I could not resolve, recorded rather than smoothed. The NONE-granularity acquisition table says Sponsored Ads is 36–98% of UniqueUsersWithPlaySessions on five games while Home is 0.1–23%. DAU by source says Home is 59–83% everywhere and Sponsored 0–2.3%. Most likely the first counts NEW users and paid traffic delivers many one-visit players who never accumulate into DAU — consistent with Sponsored's worse D7 and playtime wherever it appears. Not verified. I nearly published "Poison Treats is a paid-acquisition game" off the first table alone; the second says Home is 59.2% of its DAU.

08

What to automate

Ranked by what this study actually needed and had to do by hand.

  1. Daily grabs on every game. Technical history ages out at 30 days and RFY at ~125. Everything not captured before it ages out is gone permanently, and three games in this panel have blind periods longer than a year.
  2. Funnel-step density monitoring. A step that stops firing looks identical to a step players stopped reaching. A weekly check for steps whose non-zero rate drops below 50% would have caught this without a false alarm.
  3. Publish-triggered baselines. The pre-update snapshot is the only grab that cannot be reconstructed afterwards, and it is the one most often skipped.
  4. Feedback text classification by device. The Seal Computer finding came from 14 comments and no metric could see it. This is cheap and no dashboard does it.
  5. An objective pullback detector on the J0 rule, alerting when a game crosses it — not to predict, which fails, but to date the event consistently instead of by eye.
