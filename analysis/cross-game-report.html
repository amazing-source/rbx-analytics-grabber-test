Four games, one distribution problem

Brainrot, Seal, Verity and Waterslide are all at or near their all-time low at the same time. They did not all break at the same time. In all four, lost reach is the larger term by far. But that is the precise claim, not a clean one: conversion rose on Brainrot and Verity and fell on Seal (−35%) and Waterslide (−24%), so two of the four have a genuine conversion problem underneath the distribution one.

What the data says

tagged by how far the evidence actually goes
01

Reach is the larger term in every game — and two of four also lost conversion confirmed

DAU is the product of reach (unique users Roblox showed the game) and conversion (the share of them who played). Splitting each game's peak-to-1-Sep move into those two factors reproduces the observed DAU change to within 0.4 points in all four cases. Reach fell 56–95%. Conversion moved +14%, +6%, −24%, −35% — and on Brainrot and Verity it is higher now than it was at peak.

Reach accounts for 77–99% of the daily variance in DAU in all four games. That is an accounting result, not a causal one — DAU is defined as reach × conversion, so the decomposition says where the change came through, never why. See finding 07 for what happens when you try to push it further.

02

None of the 28 August updates caused any of this confirmed

All four studios shipped on 28 August. Testing each deploy with a trend-adjusted step model — so the decline already in progress is not credited to the update — the naive read is that Seal and Verity both took large significant hits. That read is wrong. Re-running the identical test at fake cut dates on 24–30 August produces the same "significant" step on days when nothing shipped, which means the model is fitting the curvature of a peak, not a discontinuity at a deploy.

Where the model is well specified (Brainrot and Waterslide, both on steady trends), the answer is clean: no detectable step in any engagement or monetization metric, largest effect p=0.08. In every game the turn began 4–5 days before the deploy.

03

Verity is capping its own sessions confirmed

Verity has the strongest hook in the portfolio — D1 retention of 14.6% on a 14-day mean, which is 115% above its cohort median and above the 90th percentile. It also has the shortest sessions: 8.4 minutes on the same basis, 18% below that cohort's median, and playtime per DAU below median too. Strong hook plus short sessions is the signature of a gate, not of churn.

The player feedback says exactly what the gate is. Of 2,097 comments, 8.6% mention the refill timer or message limit by name: "why do I only get like 50 questions and then wait a minute", "get rid of the message limit", and — directly costing a sale — "the messages are so limited, im not about to spend robux on a game that i wont play for the next month". Verity's cohort curve also decays fastest after D2 of any game here (5.7% → 2.7% → 1.8%).

04

Seal is losing player progress confirmed

6.3% of Seal's 318 comments report lost progress, and they are unambiguous: "doesn't save progress at all", "resets ur progress when u leave", "I lost all my progress", "I did a lot of work to make almost ALL OF THE ROAD BUT THEN I PLAY A NEW GAME BUT WHEN I JOIN BACK ITS ALL RESETED". A second cluster is a design choice rather than a bug — rebirth wipes skill upgrades, and several players ask for those to persist.

Seal also carries the worst sentiment in the set at 78% downvotes, and the lowest D1 retention at 11.8%. Given the game earns 3.5× the ARPDAU of anything else in the portfolio, this is the single most expensive defect on this page.

05

Waterslide's monetization is structurally thin confirmed

Waterslide converts more players to payers than anything else here — 3.1% of DAU on a 14-day mean, close to four times Brainrot and Verity, and 225% above its cohort median. Then it takes R$19 from each, which is 66% below the same cohort's median and a ninth of Seal's R$177. It is also 88% dependent on dev products, so there is no pass ladder catching the players who already showed they will pay.

Waterslide is also the weakest retainer in the set: D7 of 0.94% on the same 14-day basis, 31% below its cohort median, and D8 cohort retention of 0.69% against Seal's 2.12%.

06

Two of the four are running on a near-invisible audience suggestive

Age is verified for a minority of players, so these buckets are a sample of the checked minority, not the audience. With that caveat: Brainrot is 52.9% age-unchecked and Waterslide 43.5%, against Seal's 10.2%. Among checked players Brainrot skews young (20.4% under 13) and heavily male (70.3%), while Seal is evenly split by gender and spread across every age band, and Waterslide is 54.4% female.

Seal's audience being both older and better identified is consistent with it earning 3.5× the ARPDAU — older accounts have their own Robux. This is a plausible mechanism, not a measured one, and I have not tested it.

07

The data cannot prove reach caused the decline, and on the longest series it points the other way direction not established

DAU is defined as reach × conversion, so the decomposition in finding 01 is an accounting identity. It establishes that the change came through the reach term. It cannot establish that falling reach caused falling DAU, because the identity holds whichever way the causation runs, and Roblox's recommender plausibly feeds on engagement — which would make this a loop, not an arrow.

The only available direction test is lead–lag: does yesterday's change in one predict today's change in the other, controlling for the series' own lag? Run on daily log-differences over each game's last 40 days, it does not support the reach → DAU story:

A story in which these games lost players for their own reasons and the recommender responded by pulling distribution fits this evidence at least as well as the one in the banner.

08

The weekly cadence is just the weekend not supported

Brainrot shows visible weekly spikes and my first read was a Saturday event cadence. Modelling log DAU on time plus weekday dummies over eight weeks does not support that: Saturday is +6.5% against Sunday at p=0.60, i.e. indistinguishable. What is significant is the ordinary weekday trough — Tuesday −21.9% and Wednesday −25.4% against Sunday, both p<0.05. That is the normal Roblox weekend shape, not a liveops beat. Seal and Verity cannot be modelled this way at all while their launch ramp dominates the window.

Why the placebo check matters here

Without it this report would have said Seal's 28 August update cost the game 71% of its DAU and 87% of its revenue, at p=0.002. That conclusion is available from a standard, defensible test — and it is wrong. The same test says the same thing about days when nothing shipped.

What is genuinely testable: Brainrot's weekday shape (Tuesday −21.9% and Wednesday −25.4% against Sunday, both p<0.05, eight weeks of data) and its paying-user CVR step. Everything else on the deploy question is a negative result: no update in this portfolio moved a metric in a way this data can detect.

Health check

why they are all at a low, and what to do

The honest headline is that these four games have one shared cause and three separate private ones, and the shared cause is the big term.

The shared cause

All four run on Roblox Home recommendation for 62–75% of their players. Home traffic is allocated, not earned in any way a studio controls week to week, and it is the thing that collapsed: reach is down 56–95% while conversion held. Every one of these games peaked, got its algorithmic push, and then had it withdrawn on the usual schedule. Brainrot is 65 days into that and Seal is 4 days into it, which is why the portfolio looks synchronised — it is the same curve at four different offsets, not one event.

The practical consequence: no product fix will restore these DAU numbers, because nothing about the product is what changed. Product fixes change what happens to the traffic you still get, and that is where the money is.

What each game should actually do

Seal
Fix the save. This is the highest-value item on the page by a distance. Seal earns R$678k/day at 3.5× the ARPDAU of anything else in the portfolio, and players are reporting that a disconnect or a rejoin wipes their road. Its D1 of 11.8% is the lowest here and its downvote rate the highest at 78%, both consistent with new players losing a session's work. Separately, let rebirth keep skill upgrades — several players ask for exactly that, and a rebirth that wipes everything is a progression sink that reads as a bug even when it is intended.
Verity
Raise or remove the message cap. Verity's hook is the strongest asset in the portfolio — D1 above its cohort's 90th percentile — and the game is spending it on an 8.4-minute session. The cap is the single most named complaint in 2,097 comments, and at least one player states outright that it stopped them buying. Worth testing a much higher cap, or moving the paywall from how much you may talk to what you may talk about. Also worth a look: 18.8% of comments are about the AI knowing personal details, which is a retention risk and possibly a policy one.
Waterslide
Build a pass ladder. Waterslide has already solved the hard half of monetization — 3.1% of players pay, close to four times the rest of the portfolio and 225% above its cohort median. It then takes R$19 from each of them, 66% below its cohort median, with 88% of revenue coming from one-shot dev products. There is a proven willingness to pay here with nothing above R$19 to spend it on. Its D7 is also the weakest in the set, so a reason to come back on day 3 is the second item.
Brainrot
Decide whether to keep funding it. 65 days post-peak, down 93.7%, below its cohort median on D1, session length, playtime and ARPPU, and still bleeding 3.8% a day. Nothing here is broken and nothing here is winning. The one asset worth noting is that 20.5% of its players come from friends, the highest in the portfolio and the only channel a studio can actually influence — if anything gets funded, it should be whatever makes that number bigger.

Best fundamentals, and worst structure

Best unit economics: Seal — trend data too short to trust yet. It earns 3.5× the ARPDAU of any other game here, holds the best D7 and the best D8 cohort retention, and clears its benchmark cohort on every metric measured. That is a claim about fundamentals only. On trajectory it is unrateable: 4 post-peak days, week-over-week figures inflated by a launch ramp sitting inside the comparison window, the fastest current slide in the set at −25.9% a day, and a save bug. Calling it the healthiest game outright would not survive contact with any of those.

Sliding worst: Waterslide, on structure rather than speed. It has the slowest decay in the set at −2.8% a day, but it is 30 days post-peak with the weakest retention here (D7 0.94%, D8 cohort 0.69%) and ARPPU two thirds below its cohort median. Brainrot is further gone in absolute terms — 93.7% off peak — but it is at least mid-pack on the rates. Waterslide is the one whose fundamentals will not hold up the next time Home gives it a push.

What this analysis cannot tell you

Why the reach fell, or even whether it fell first. The RFY ranking signals are captured and none is falling hard enough on its own to explain a 56–95% reach loss, and the ranking function is not published. Worse, the lead–lag test in finding 07 cannot separate reach → DAU from DAU → reach, and on Brainrot it leans the wrong way for this report. "The algorithm withdrew the push" is a description of the shape, not a mechanism and not a proven direction. Treat it as the question to answer, not the answer.

Also open: the country-level ARPDAU test that would confirm the market-mix explanation for the revenue gap, and whether Seal's save loss is a bug or a data-store design limit. Both are answerable with grabs the current plan does not make.
